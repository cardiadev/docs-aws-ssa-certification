## Overview

A **VPN (Virtual Private Network)** allows you to securely connect your on-premises network to your private resources in AWS. Traffic is sent through an **encrypted IPsec tunnel** that travels over the internet.

## Components

| Component | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **VPN Gateway** (VGW) | AWS side | Terminates the VPN tunnel on AWS; attached to a VPC |
| **Customer Gateway** (CGW) | On-premises side | Terminates the VPN tunnel on your network; requires a public IP |
| **IPsec tunnel** | Internet | Encrypted connection between VGW and CGW |

## How it Works

1. Deploy a **VPN Gateway** and attach it to your VPC
2. Configure a **Customer Gateway** on your on-premises network with a public IP
3. An **encrypted IPsec tunnel** is established between both endpoints over the internet

## Routing

Two options for AWS to learn your on-premises routes:

- **Static** — manually define a route in the AWS route table (e.g. `192.168.0.0/16` → VPN Gateway)
- **Dynamic** — use **BGP** to automatically exchange routes between the VGW and CGW

## Limits and Performance

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Max bandwidth** per tunnel | 1.25 Gbps |
| **Packets per second** | 140,000 |
| **MTU** | 1,446 bytes |

You can use **ECMP** (Equal-Cost Multi-Path) with multiple VPN tunnels to increase total bandwidth.

## Pricing

- Charged per **VPN connection hour** available
- Charged for **egress data** out of AWS

<Callout type="warn">
**For the SAA-C03 exam:** The VPN Gateway is on the AWS side and the Customer Gateway is on the on-premises side. Traffic travels encrypted over the internet (unlike Direct Connect). Max bandwidth per tunnel is 1.25 Gbps. You can use static or dynamic (BGP) routing.
</Callout>
